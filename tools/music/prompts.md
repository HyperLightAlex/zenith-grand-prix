# Suno briefs

What each piece of the game's recorded music should be, as written for
Suno's custom mode: the Styles box, the Exclude box (Advanced Options; a
few closely related sounds work better than a long list) and, for the
shape, section tags in the Lyrics box with no words. Kept here so the
next one is made from what the last ones taught.

What the game needs from every one of them: one steady tempo; the full
sound early (a screen's music is heard from its first seconds); no long
fade in or out; a stretch in the middle that can loop (not the hangar's:
see below). Stems exported as WAVs, plus the full song if it can be (the
build matches its mastering).

## Race songs: what each needs

Instrumental, one steady tempo, the full band from the first bar, about
2:30 to 3:00. The race builds it lap by lap from its stems: lap 1 is the
melody and the bass (so the lead should carry the song without drums),
lap 2 brings in the drums, lap 3 everything (so give it percussion and
effects of its own, which come out as clean stems and have something to
add). It loops a long stretch of one energy from its middle (about a lap:
60 to 75 s), then plays its ending, so it needs a clear one: a cadence
onto a final hit, not a fade. A lap-3 finale is a bonus: a last chorus a
step up (Canyon) or a build and drop (Helios). Keep the lead on one
instrument: vocal chops and choirs get carved out of the other stems and
tangle them. Export the stems and the full song as WAVs. The circuits'
songs so far are in A, G, E, D, C# and C minor, so a new one takes another
key (Zenith Ring's is asked for in B minor).

## Frostline race

The fifth circuit: a night race over an ice shelf under the aurora, out to
a glacier and back (the Aurora Split, the Ice Barrel up into the glacier,
the Calving off its face, the Icefall and the Aurora Loop). Cold, clear and
fast: crystal and glass rather than Canyon's grit or Helios's trance. Lap
3 is the last chorus a semitone up (the aurora flaring).

Title: 極光ライン (Aurora Line)

Styles:

```
1980s mecha anime racing BGM, instrumental, 150 BPM, F# minor, crystalline FM bell arpeggios, glassy synth lead carrying a soaring hook, icy analog pads, driving octave synth bass, punchy gated reverb drums, tambourine and shaker, whooshing risers and sweeps, a night race across an ice shelf under the aurora, fast, cold and determined, bright and clear, tight modern mix
```

Exclude: `vocals, choir, saxophone`

Lyrics (structure only):

```
[Intro: full band from the first bar, the hook on the synth lead]
[Verse]
[Pre-Chorus]
[Chorus]
[Verse]
[Pre-Chorus]
[Chorus]
[Bridge: bells and pads over the beat, the drums keep going]
[Final Chorus: key change up a semitone, everything in]
[Outro: cadence onto a final hit]
```

## Titan's Rest race

The sixth circuit: a sunrise race on and through a colossal war machine
fallen in a white desert, up its arm, over its face, through its halo,
inside its ribs and twice round the blade of its sword. Heroic and vast:
a 1980s super robot show's battle music, heavier and slower than the
others, with a desert colour in its melody. Lap 3 is a build into the
biggest section (the giant's theme at full strength).

## Zenith Ring race

The seventh circuit and the season's finale: a race in orbit round a great
ring station over the Earth, up the inside of the Ring and down its outside
upside down, round the core in the Möbius, down the Mass Driver and through
the docking ring, and over the solar arrays with nothing past their edge.
The biggest and fastest of them: a 1980s space-opera mecha show's climax
battle music, soaring and bright, the sense of height and speed of orbit.
Lap 3 is the last chorus a whole step up, everything in (the final lap of
the season).

## Title theme

Made: Blue Night Sky. What it was asked for: it should sound like the
opening, anime-opening J-rock with guitars and a synth lead, fast and
hopeful, not a brass fanfare. (A Cover of the opening with Instrumental
Only on is the other way there: it keeps the opening's melody.)

Styles:

```
anime opening theme, instrumental J-rock, 150 BPM, driving palm-muted electric guitars opening into big power chords, bright synth lead carrying a soaring hook, punchy live drums, melodic picked bass, hopeful and fast, wind and a blue night sky, mecha racing anime, 90s anime OP energy, tight modern mix
```

Exclude: `brass fanfare, orchestral, marching snare`

## Hangar: a setlist

In the game (see README.md, "The hangar's music"); more can join it. Not one song on repeat: a handful of 1980s-flavoured tracks, played one
after another under the hangar, setup, briefing and lobby screens, that the
player can skip through, like the pit crew's radio. Background music: mid
to low energy, nothing that builds to a climax. Each plays through once,
so it needs no loop, and a natural ending is fine (the next one follows).
2:00 to 3:00 each. They share a base, and each adds its own flavour, so
they belong together without sounding alike.

Exclude for all of them: `saxophone, jazz, rock drums`

1. City pop, night drive:

```
1980s Japanese city pop instrumental, background music, 108 BPM, slap bass, bright FM electric piano, clean funk guitar chords, gated reverb drums, glossy synth stabs, relaxed and cool, night drive along the bay
```

2. Synthwave, neon garage:

```
1980s synthwave instrumental, background music, 100 BPM, warm analog pads, arpeggiated synth bass, drum machine, soft lead synth melody, neon-lit garage at night, laid-back and steady
```

3. 80s mecha anime, workshop:

```
1980s mecha anime background music, instrumental, 96 BPM, analog synth ostinato, FM bell melody, synth bass, electronic toms, calm focus, technicians tuning machines, understated
```

4. 80s anime ending, evening:

```
1980s anime ending theme, instrumental, 92 BPM, soft synth lead, DX7 electric piano, chorus-drenched clean guitar, gentle drum machine, wistful and warm, evening light over the track
```

5. Italo-tinged cruise (the liveliest; still background):

```
1980s italo disco instrumental, background music, 112 BPM, octave synth bass, drum machine, shimmering arpeggios, simple synth hook, cruising, light and steady, no build
```

Lyrics (structure only), for any of them:

```
[Intro]
[Main Theme]
[Variation]
[Main Theme]
[Outro: gentle ending]
```

## Results, loss

Made: Minor Theme. Plays on the results when the player's team loses the round, and on the
Grand Prix award below first place. The other half of トロフィーセレモニー
(G major): E minor is its relative minor. Synth-led and plainly 80s anime:
the music under an episode's ending after a defeat. It stays sad; no lift
into a hopeful major (the first try had one, and a horn, and too little
synth).

Title: 次のスタートライン (The Next Start Line)

Styles:

```
1980s anime defeat theme, instrumental, synth-led, 88 BPM, E minor, analog synth pads, mournful lead synth melody with vibrato, DX7 electric piano, fretless synth bass, slow gated reverb drums, melancholic and heavy-hearted, rain on the pit lane at night, mecha anime episode ending after a loss, stays in minor to the end
```

Exclude: `French horn, orchestral strings, major key`

Lyrics (structure only):

```
[Intro: synth pads, the lead melody from the first bar]
[Theme]
[Theme: drums and bass join]
[Bridge: darker]
[Theme]
[Outro: ends on the minor chord]
```

Straight in, no fade. 1:30 to 2:00.
